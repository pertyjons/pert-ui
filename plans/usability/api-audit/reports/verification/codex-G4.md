## My findings disputed

- **codex-T1-3 — maintain.** Claude’s statement that no plausibly useful ordering gap was found does not dispose of the cited instances. `Capacity` stores an ordered integer (`C/live/mod.rs:183`); `PowerExponent`, `Magnification`, `UiZoom`, `ViewScale` and `PresentationRate` reject non-finite or nonpositive/out-of-range values (`C/parameter.rs:76`; `R/pointer.rs:116`; `R/runtime/settings.rs:39`; `R/transform.rs:291`; `R/runtime/presentation.rs:17`). Their missing ordering/equality/hash implementations are supported by those domains. Rechecking the listed exact-data records also supports conditional `Eq`; raw floating-point payloads remain excluded.

- **T2 “no findings” disposition — maintain for established ownership requirements.** `Divider` and `Svg` have no runtime-affine fields, while `RichText` and `TextSpan` can contain explicitly UI-local font identities. Their auto traits fit their representations. Family-wide `!Send` markers or a sendable styled-text form require policy/design decisions; missing thread prose alone is outside this audit’s documentation scope.

- **codex-T4-1 — maintain; exception remains a policy question.** Returning the rejected input is deliberate (`C/family.rs:56,66`), but neither carrier implements `Error`. The current rubric requires that trait (`plans/usability/api-audit/rubric.md:324`). A standard-library analogy does not itself establish a project exception.

- **codex-T5-1 — maintain the finding; narrow the severity claim.** Contrary to Claude’s blanket classification of the remaining public-field structs as unconstrained data, these fields promise relationships: ordered actions, no disabled actions and no protected value (`R/diagnostics.rs:350,476,481,494`). Public assignment bypasses the builders, and inspection forwards the result (`R/runtime/inspect.rs:145,185`). The evidence demonstrates exposure from invalid custom semantics, not a built-in password-control leak. I would rate the disclosure risk **medium**, with the action/name inconsistencies low.
